I've heard it said that JOB is an acronym for "Jail Of Boss." The first time I heard that, it stuck with me — because it names something a lot of people feel but rarely say out loud.

You don't have to hate your job for that feeling to be true. You can like your coworkers, respect your boss, and still feel like your calendar belongs to someone else.

Here's the thing nobody tells you at the start: escaping that doesn't require a dramatic leap. Almost every lifestyle-business owner I've interviewed for Terrific 30 started the exact same way — small, on the side, while still collecting a paycheck somewhere else.

## What Actually Changes First

It's not the income. It's the identity. The moment you start building something of your own — a blog, a small paid community, a simple digital product — you stop being purely an employee in your own head, even before the numbers say you can quit anything.

That shift is worth more than people give it credit for. It's the difference between waiting for permission and giving it to yourself.

## Start Smaller Than Feels Reasonable

If you're waiting for the "right" idea before you start, you'll wait forever. Start with the smallest possible version of the thing:

- One article instead of a whole blog strategy
- One audience of ten people instead of ten thousand
- One offer instead of a whole business

The people I've talked to who actually built something rarely started with a grand plan. They started with a page, and they kept it.

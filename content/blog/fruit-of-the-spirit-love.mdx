> “²²But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith,
> ²³Meekness, temperance: against such there is no law.”
>
> — Galatians 5:22-23 KJV

The very first characteristic listed among the 9 in the fruit of the Spirit is love.

The Greek word used here is Agapē (ἀγάπη). (God's Love)

## Agapē (God's Love) Is NOT Just Feelings Or Emotions!

A lot of people think love is just feelings or emotions, that is just so wrong.

Agapē is much more than a feeling or an emotion. It is a deliberate choice to seek the good of another person, even when it requires sacrifice.

This is the kind of love God has shown toward us.

> “But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us.”
>
> — Romans 5:8 KJV

Jesus demonstrated Agapē love by willingly laying down His life on the cross for our sins.

As Christians, we are called to love others with that same kind of selfless love.

## Four Greek Words For Love

The New Testament uses several Greek words to describe different kinds of love.

### Agapē (ἀγάπη) — God's Unconditional, Sacrificial Love

A love that chooses to seek another person's good, regardless of feelings and emotions.

This is the word used in Galatians 5:22.

It is often characterized by:

- Selflessness
- Sacrifice
- Forgiveness
- Grace
- Mercy
- Compassion
- Unconditional commitment
- Seeking the good of others
- Loving even when it costs us something

### Philia (φιλία) — Brotherly Love / Friendship Love

Brotherly love or friendship.

The affection and loyalty shared between close friends.

(This is where we get the name Philadelphia — the "City of Brotherly Love.")

It is often characterized by:

- Friendship
- Loyalty
- Trust
- Mutual respect
- Fellowship
- Encouragement
- Companionship
- Shared interests
- Faithfulness to friends

### Storgē (στοργή) — Family Love / Fatherly Love

Natural family affection.

The love between parents and children, brothers and sisters, and close family members.

The exact noun storgē does not appear in the New Testament, although related forms such as philostorgos ("kindly affectionate") appear in passages like Romans 12:10.

It is often characterized by:

- Affection
- Care
- Protection
- Nurturing
- Commitment
- Loyalty
- Patience
- Compassion
- The natural bond between parents, children, brothers, and sisters

### Erōs (ἔρως) — Erotic / Romantic / Marital Love

Erotic, romantic or marital love.

Interestingly, the Greek word erōs itself does not appear in the New Testament.

However, the Bible clearly celebrates the love between husband and wife in places such as the Song of Solomon and Hebrews 13:4.

It is often characterized by:

- Romantic affection
- Attraction
- Desire
- Intimacy
- Passion
- Exclusivity
- Commitment within marriage
- Physical affection
- The one-flesh relationship between husband and wife

While the Bible teaches about all these kinds of love, the type of love mentioned in the fruit of the Spirit is Agapē: God's love, unconditional, selfless, sacrificial love. This is the kind of love that the Holy Spirit desires to produce in every believer's life.

## "The Greatest Of These Is Love"

The Apostle Paul devoted an entire chapter to explaining the importance of love.

He wrote:

> “If I speak in the tongues of men or of angels, but do not have love, I am only a resounding gong or a clanging cymbal.”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 13:1 NIV

Later he concludes:

> “¹³And now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love.”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 13:13 NIV

Paul is teaching that no matter how gifted, knowledgeable, or successful we become, if we lack Christlike love, we have missed what matters most.
